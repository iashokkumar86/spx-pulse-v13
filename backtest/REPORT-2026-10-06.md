# V13 backtest report — 2026-10-06

Source: TradingView Deep Backtest of `SPX_Pulse_V13_strategy.pine` on SP:SPX
3-min regular candles, 2016-07-05 → 2026-10-06. 3,847 trades over 1,811
sessions (2.1/day). Export: `PulseV13-BT_SP_SPX_2026-10-06.csv` (not committed).

**Variant caveat:** this run used order size 1, so the +1R scale-half closed
the ENTIRE position — it tests a "full exit at +1R" variant, not the V13
scale+trail engine. Costs were zero. Timestamps exported in UTC-5, so the
time-of-day table is smeared ±1h across DST.

## Headline

| | n | win% | avg/trade | PF | max DD |

| All trades, all 10.3 yrs | 3,847 | 53.6% | +0.001% | **1.02** | — |

**The full mechanical system is breakeven before costs over a decade.** With
even 0.02% round-trip friction (≈1.3 SPX pts) the OOS baseline degrades to
PF 0.72, −23% cumulative. Realistic SPXW 0DTE vertical friction is several
times that. As a fully mechanical options system at default settings, V13 is
not viable; its live value has been decision support plus the operator's
selectivity.

## Handoff questions

**Q1 — per-setup expectancy (tag × regime, full period, pre-cost):**
Positive, meaningful n: ORB/TU (PF 1.38, n 228), MOMO/TU (1.19, n 176),
FLAG/TD (1.38, n 124), TREND/TU (1.08, n 1037), TREND/TD (1.07, n 686).
Negative: MOMO/TD (0.72), FLAG/TU (0.79), BRKOUT everywhere (0.84, n 206),
GAPFADE (0.80, n 52), ORB outside TU (0.63–0.94), everything in RANGE (0.92)
and UNDECIDED (0.93) regimes. The system is a **with-trend continuation
system**; its counter-trend and range modules destroy value as coded.

Walk-forward check: cells selected on in-sample only (PF>1.05, n≥100 →
ORB/TU, MOMO/TU, TREND/TD) held up out-of-sample: PF 1.09, +0.0066%/trade,
57.1% win (n 361). Real but tiny — ≈0.4 SPX pts/trade, below any realistic
cost line (see Q-costs).

**Q2 — minimumScore:** the score is NOT monotonically calibrated. Full-period
PF by bucket: 70–74 → 1.02, 75–79 → 0.92, 80–84 → 1.08, 85+ → 0.95. Raising
the threshold to 75 makes things worse; 80 is defensible (IS 1.13 → OOS 1.15),
85 is noise (IS 0.85 vs OOS 1.41, n 52). Recommendation: keep 70 for signal
generation, treat 80–84 as the quality band, and fix the score weights rather
than the threshold — the setup-type and regime terms carry the information;
the rest appears to add noise.

**Q3 — minRewardRisk:** printed RR is inversely related to outcome
(RR 1.5–2 → PF 1.12; 3–5 → 0.94). The 1.5 floor is fine; there is no case for
raising it, and capping max RR did not improve OOS. Printed R is a room
ceiling, not an expectancy predictor — consistent with its definition.

**Q4 — EM anti-chase cutoffs:** not isolated by this variant (target
irrelevant when exiting at 1R). Defer to the qty-2 rerun.

**Q5 — regime gate:** the gate's directional calls are the single most useful
component — the same setups flip sign across regimes (MOMO: 1.19 in TU vs
0.72 in TD-counter use). But UNDECIDED (n 566, PF 0.93) and RANGE (n 325,
0.92) trades lose: the +5 UNDECIDED score bump is insufficient. Recommendation
to test: no entries at all in UNDECIDED, and range-regime trading limited to
LVL only (LVL/RG was the least-bad range cell at 0.93) or disabled.

**Q6 — exit engine:** not yet tested (variant caveat). What this run does
show: STOP exits average −0.179% vs 1R wins +0.147% — losers are 22% larger
than winners because stops fire on close-through-level; 610 of 3,847 trades
(16%) lost more than 1.2× the average win. Before re-running, consider adding
a touch-stop input to the port; capping that tail is worth more than any
threshold tweak. TIMESTOP is neutral (PF 1.04, n 658) — it costs nothing and
frees capital: keep. 15:30 flush trades are net losers (PF 0.68, n 179).

**Q7 — session windows:** entries in the last tradable hour are weak and
trades still open at 15:30 lose on average → an earlier entry cutoff
(~14:00 ET) is worth testing on the qty-2 rerun. The lunch-haircut question is
confounded by the DST smear in this export; re-test with the chart timezone
pinned to America/New_York.

**Q-costs:** avg win is 0.133% (≈6–7 SPX pts at current prices). OOS filtered
expectancy +0.0066%: a 0.02% round trip flips it to PF 0.84; 0.04% → 0.63.
**No tested configuration survives realistic 0DTE option friction.** Matches
the 2026-08-31 options-data audit conclusion ("research/shadow-only").

# Update (same day): qty-2 reruns — exit engine A/B and final verdict

Three further Deep Backtests with order size 2 (real scale-half), 2016-07 →
2026-10. "Trimmed" = touch stops + no UNDECIDED entries + with-trend-only.

| Configuration | n | PF | OOS PF (70/30) | avg/trade | PF at 0.02% cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Official V13 defaults | 3,483 | 1.05 | 1.03 | +0.0037% | 0.80 |
| 1R-full-exit variant (earlier run) | 3,847 | 1.02 | 1.00 | +0.0010% | 0.72 |
| Trimmed, V13 scale+trail | 2,449 | 1.17 | 1.08 | +0.0116% | 0.89 |
| **Trimmed, fixed stop-and-target** | 2,205 | **1.25** | **1.13** | **+0.0204%** | 1.00 |

## Q6 answered: the scale-half + trail engine costs money

Same entries, fixed target vs scale+trail: PF 1.25 vs 1.17 (OOS 1.13 vs
1.08), expectancy 1.75x higher. Banking half at +1R and trailing the rest
systematically sells the winners the system needs; in fixed mode even the
15:30 forced exits are strongly positive (+0.154% avg) because full size is
still on when trend days close at their highs. Win rate drops (53% → 45%)
but target wins average +0.33%. **Recommendation: plain structure stop +
structure/EM target, no scaling.**

## Q4/Q5 refinements that replicated across IS and OOS

Robust cells: TREND/TU (IS 1.16 → OOS 1.48), TREND/TD (1.15 → 1.15),
ORB/TU (1.81 → 1.20). Consistently negative: MOMO/TD (0.85 → 0.57) — cut it.
FLAG and ORB/TD flip sign between halves: no verdict. Touch stops confirmed
better than close-through (loss tail 17.4% → 13.3% of trades).

Score calibration confirmed broken in every run: 80–84 is the best bucket,
75–79 and 85+ at or below breakeven. The threshold is not the lever; the
weights are.

## Final verdict

Best honest configuration (trimmed roster, fixed exits) earns
**+0.009%/trade out-of-sample (~0.06R) — breakeven at 0.02% round-trip cost
and negative at realistic SPXW 0DTE friction (0.04%+).** Ten years of data,
four configurations, one conclusion: the signal engine has a real but small
with-trend edge on the underlying, and no tested configuration clears option
execution costs mechanically.

V13 should remain what it was designed to be: a decision-support study. The
numbers say the operator's discretion must add at least ~0.03-0.05% per trade
(a few SPX points of selectivity/timing) for the combined system to profit —
which matches the live experience of trading it selectively rather than
taking every signal.

Live-study changes these results do justify:
1. Exit rule: drop the 1R scale-half; hold full size to the structure target
   (keep the time stop — it was net positive in every run).
2. Treat stops as touch levels, not close-through.
3. Skip UNDECIDED-regime signals and MOMO puts in TREND DOWN.
4. Trust the 80-84 score band most; do not chase 85+ prints.

# Update 2026-10-14: overnight-bias test (concept from Saty's premarket framing)

Hypothesis: premarket direction (SPY extended hours, >=0.3% from prior RTH
close, closing in the directional quarter of the premarket range) should feed
the regime gate at the open. Tested as +2 votes until 11:00, control = the
vote-tagged 2016-2026 run (sanity: 1,978 trades identical on non-bias days).

| | n | PF | IS / OOS |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Overall, bias ON (matched period) | 3,276 | 1.18 | vs control 1.21 — slightly WORSE |
| Trades WITH the bias | 664 | 1.16 | 1.15 / 1.16 — merely average |
| Trades AGAINST the bias | 113 | **0.88** | **0.91 / 0.81 — consistently negative** |

Verdict: bias **votes rejected** (double-counts the gap module's evidence,
promotes mediocre setups). Bias as a **veto ships as V13.26**: a manual
morning input (`overnightBias` = up/down/none, feed has no premarket) that
suppresses opposing signals until 11:00 and never adds trades. Same evidence
class as the MOMO/TD removal: a both-halves-negative cohort, excised.
